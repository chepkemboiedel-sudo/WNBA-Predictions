How missing players affect the forecast

Player effects are shrunken associations between participation and score residuals in 565 completed regular-season games from 2025–26. Only teams with complete current-season player history are adjusted.

Confirmed season-long absences carry through the remaining bracket. Fresh game-specific “Out” reports apply only to the next scheduled game within 72 hours. Older reports contradicted by later appearances, ambiguous roster assignments, and questionable or probable statuses do not trigger an assumed absence.

Estimated penalties are reduced by the player's regular-season participation rate to avoid counting existing absences twice. Effects are capped at four points per player and six per team. These are conservative modeling choices, not measured causal effects.

The availability layer is experimental. Historical calibration and validation on this page describe the score-only model. They do not establish injury-adjusted accuracy. Roster changes determine report assignment; transfers do not receive an unverified automatic boost.

Minute restrictions and mid-game injuries are not forecast.

Under the hood

Score-only baseline validation

Reading it fairly

What the model can't see

  • Player effects are experimental. Current rosters and reported absences are used conservatively; the availability layer has not been independently calibrated. Uncertain reports do not imply confirmed absences.
  • Playoff rest is neutralized. The sim doesn't model a team resting after a sweep or grinding through a decisive Game 3.
  • Seeding tiebreakers are simplified (win% then point differential), not the league's full head-to-head chain before the playoffs. Once available, actual first-round pairings resolve tied seeds.
  • Completed results are fixed. Only unplayed playoff games are simulated. Finals: Golden State Valkyries leads Atlanta Dream 3–2.
