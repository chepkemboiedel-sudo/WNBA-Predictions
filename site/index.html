How missing players affect the forecast

Player effects are shrunken associations between participation and score residuals in 567 completed regular-season games from the current and preceding season. Only teams with complete current-season player history are adjusted.

Confirmed season-long absences carry through the remaining bracket. Fresh game-specific “Out” reports apply only to the next scheduled game within 72 hours. Older reports contradicted by later appearances, ambiguous roster assignments, and questionable or probable statuses do not trigger an assumed absence.

Estimated penalties are reduced by the player's regular-season participation rate to avoid counting existing absences twice. Effects are capped at four points per player and six per team. These are conservative modeling choices, not measured causal effects.

The availability layer is experimental. Historical calibration and validation on this page describe the score-only model. They do not establish injury-adjusted accuracy. Roster changes determine report assignment; transfers do not receive an unverified automatic boost.

Fresh, explicitly stated numeric minute limits can receive a fractional next-game penalty. Live context shows projected rotations and a separate illustrative mid-game injury scenario. These additions require their own prospective validation.

Coverage, method, and limits

202 of 326 eligible games have explicit pregame reports for both teams. 153 of 155 report files passed validation. Coverage selects which games can be evaluated, so compare the two models within the same sample.

The score-only model was trained on 2016–2025 games and evaluated on 2026. Player effects were fitted at the start of each month using only preceding games. Only confirmed Out/Suspended statuses were used, with the existing participation discount and effect caps; no settings were tuned to these outcomes.

Reports were selected at 09:00 ET for tips before 16:00 ET, otherwise 15:00 ET, at least an hour before tip. Missing or unsubmitted reports kept the baseline. An incomplete player archive disabled the affected team's adjustment in 56 covered games; 7 player-game Out records could not be uniquely matched and received no invented effect.

Only 5 covered games were playoff games. This test does not validate series or championship probabilities. It tests the numerical adjustment with official archived reports, rather than recreating the complete historical ESPN injury and roster feed. Bootstrap intervals are approximate and do not guarantee performance in future seasons.

Historical and prospective evidence stay separate. Existing historical rows are frozen. Newly evaluated historical games use archived pregame reports and monthly past-only fitting, but are evaluated after the game. The prospective trial records paired production game probabilities 5 minutes to 24 hours before scheduled tip, using a verified player snapshot fetched within two hours. It retains the original model hash and roster/injury evidence. Only recorded predictions are scored when the matching game finishes; missed captures are never backfilled.

The prospective trial uses fixed regular-season form, while the historical test uses pregame running form. Daily sampling can miss early games or late injury updates. New cohorts start empty. Progress checkpoints are cumulative, not independent tests; repeated viewing does not establish significance or automatically enable injuries.

Reading it fairly

What the model can't see

  • Player effects are experimental. Current rosters and reported absences are used conservatively; the availability layer has not been independently calibrated. Uncertain reports do not imply confirmed absences.
  • Playoff rest is neutralized. The sim doesn't model a team resting after a sweep or grinding through a decisive Game 3.
  • Seeding tiebreakers are simplified (win% then point differential), not the league's full head-to-head chain before the playoffs. Once available, actual first-round pairings resolve tied seeds.
  • Completed results are fixed. Only unplayed playoff games are simulated. Golden State Valkyries won the Finals 4–3.
